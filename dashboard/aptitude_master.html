🔗 HCF & LCM

Highest Common Factor & Least Common Multiple — tested in almost every exam.

Core Concepts

HCF (Highest Common Factor)

The largest number that divides all given numbers exactly. Also called GCD (Greatest Common Divisor).

LCM (Least Common Multiple)

The smallest number that is exactly divisible by all given numbers.

HCF × LCM = Product of the two numbers
(For two numbers only!)

HCF(a,b) × LCM(a,b) = a × b
How to Find

Prime Factorisation Method

For HCF: Find prime factors of each number. Take the lowest power of common factors.
For LCM: Take the highest power of all prime factors.

Example: HCF and LCM of 12 and 18

12 = 2² × 3¹
18 = 2¹ × 3²

HCF = 2¹ × 3¹ = 6 (lowest powers of common factors)
LCM = 2² × 3² = 36 (highest powers of all factors)

Verify: 6 × 36 = 216 = 12 × 18 ✓
⚡ Tricks & Shortcuts
🎯Word Problem: "rings/bells/lights meet again"▼

If bells ring every A and B seconds respectively, they ring together after LCM(A, B) seconds.

Example: Bells ring every 6 and 15 seconds. When do they ring together?
LCM(6,15) = 30 seconds.
🔥Largest tile / box / container → HCF▼

To find the largest measure that can exactly measure given quantities → use HCF.

Example: Largest rope to measure 48m and 60m exactly? HCF(48,60) = 12m
💡Numbers leaving same remainder▼

If N divided by A and B leaves same remainder R: HCF of (N-R) gives divisors.

If numbers A, B, C when divided by D give same remainder R: D = HCF(A-B, B-C, A-C)

Example: Find number that divides 25, 37, 61 and leaves remainder 1.
25-1=24, 37-1=36, 61-1=60 → HCF(24,36,60) = 12

Master this and Profit/Loss, Interest, and Data Interpretation become trivial.

Core Formulas
Percentage = (Part / Whole) × 100

Part = (Percentage × Whole) / 100

% Change = [(New Value - Old Value) / Old Value] × 100

⚡ Tricks & Shortcuts
🎯Successive Percentage Change (Net Effect)▼

If a value first increases by A%, then by B%, the net % change is:

Net % = A + B + (A×B)/100

Use negative values for decrease.

Example: Price increased 20%, then 10%. Net?
20 + 10 + (20×10)/100 = 30 + 2 = 32% increase
Example: Price increased 20%, then decreased 20%. Net?
20 + (-20) + (20×-20)/100 = 0 - 4 = 4% decrease
⚠️ Not zero! Always use the formula.
🔥If A is X% of B, what % is B of A?▼
B is (100/X × 100)% of A = 10000/X %
Example: A is 25% of B. What % is B of A?
10000/25 = 400%
💡Population Growth / Depreciation▼
Final Value = Initial × (1 ± R/100)^T
Example: Population 10000, grows 5% per year. After 2 years?
10000 × (1.05)² = 10000 × 1.1025 = 11025

📈 Profit & Loss

Essential for every competitive exam. Learn to solve in seconds.

Key Terms & Formulas
CP = Cost Price (what you pay to buy)
SP = Selling Price (what you get when you sell)
MP = Marked Price (price on the tag)

Profit = SP - CP | Loss = CP - SP
Profit% = (Profit/CP) × 100
Loss% = (Loss/CP) × 100

SP = CP × (100 + Profit%) / 100
SP = CP × (100 - Loss%) / 100
Golden Rule: Profit/Loss % is ALWAYS calculated on CP, unless stated otherwise.
⚡ Tricks & Shortcuts
🎯Multiplier Method (Fastest!)▼

Convert % to a multiplier. No formula memorization needed!

SP = CP × Multiplier
25% profit → multiply by 1.25
20% loss → multiply by 0.80
CP = SP / Multiplier
Example: CP = ₹400, sell at 15% profit. SP?
SP = 400 × 1.15 = ₹460
Example: SP = ₹340, sold at 15% loss. CP?
CP = 340 / 0.85 = ₹400
🔥Dishonest Shopkeeper Trick▼

If a shopkeeper sells at CP but uses false weights (claims X grams, gives Y grams):

Profit% = (X - Y) / Y × 100
Example: Claims to sell 1 kg but gives 900g. Profit%?
(1000-900)/900 × 100 = 100/900 × 100 = 11.11%
💡Equal Profit-Loss % → Always a Loss!▼

If one article sold at X% profit and another at X% loss:

Net Loss% = X² / 100
Example: Two items sold at 10% profit and 10% loss each.
Net = 10²/100 = 1% loss overall!
⚡Discount & Marked Price▼
SP = MP × (1 - Discount%/100)
Discount% = (MP - SP)/MP × 100
Trick: To find overall profit/loss when both markup and discount are given:
If MP = CP × 1.40 (40% markup) and Discount = 20%:
SP = MP × 0.8 = CP × 1.40 × 0.8 = CP × 1.12 → 12% profit

🏦 Simple & Compound Interest

Bank and finance problems made lightning-fast.

Formulas
SIMPLE INTEREST
SI = (P × R × T) / 100
Amount = P + SI = P(1 + RT/100)

COMPOUND INTEREST
A = P × (1 + R/100)^T
CI = A - P

P = Principal, R = Rate%, T = Time (years)
⚡ Tricks & Shortcuts
🎯SI vs CI for 2 years (key difference)▼
CI - SI (for 2 years) = P × (R/100)²
Example: P=10000, R=10%, T=2. Find CI-SI.
10000 × (10/100)² = 10000 × 0.01 = ₹100
🔥Rule of 72 — Doubling Time▼

Approximate years for money to double under CI:

Years to double ≈ 72 / R
At 8% per year: 72/8 = 9 years to double
💡Effective Rate for Half-Yearly / Quarterly▼

When compounding is more frequent, adjust R and T:

Half-yearly: R → R/2, T → 2T
Quarterly: R → R/4, T → 4T
Example: 10% per annum, compounded half-yearly, T=1 year.
A = P × (1 + 5/100)² = P × 1.1025
Effective rate = 10.25%

⚖️ Ratio & Proportion

The language of comparison. Master this for mixtures, partnerships, and more.

Key Concepts

Ratio

Ratio a:b means for every 'a' parts of X, there are 'b' parts of Y. Ratios can be scaled: 2:3 = 4:6 = 10:15.

Proportion

If a:b = c:d, then a×d = b×c (cross multiplication). This is called "Product of extremes = Product of means".

If ratio is a:b, actual values are ax and bx
(where x is the common multiplier)

If a:b = c:d → a/b = c/d → ad = bc
⚡ Tricks & Shortcuts
🎯Divide a sum in given ratio — instantly▼

To divide total T in ratio a:b:c...

First share = T × a/(a+b+c)
Second share = T × b/(a+b+c)
Example: Divide ₹1200 in ratio 3:4:5.
Total parts = 12
A = 1200×3/12 = 300, B = 400, C = 500
🔥Mixture / Alligation Rule▼

To mix two items of different prices to get a target price:

Cheap (C) Expensive (E)
\ /
Target (T)
/ \
(E-T) (T-C)

Ratio = (E-T) : (T-C)
Example: Mix rice at ₹20/kg and ₹30/kg to get ₹24/kg.
Ratio = (30-24):(24-20) = 6:4 = 3:2
💡Third Proportional & Mean Proportional▼
Mean proportional of a,b = √(a×b)
Third proportional of a,b = b²/a
Mean proportional of 4 and 16 = √(4×16) = √64 = 8

🔧 Time & Work

Learn the LCM method — the fastest approach used by toppers.

The Golden Rule
If a person completes work in N days:
Work done per day = 1/N

If A does 1/A and B does 1/B per day:
Together they do (1/A + 1/B) per day
Time to finish = 1 / (1/A + 1/B) = AB/(A+B)
⚡ LCM Method (Superfast!)

Step-by-step LCM method

Assume total work = LCM of all given days. Convert days to efficiency (work per day). Then solve directly — no fractions!

Example: A can do in 12 days, B in 8 days. Together?

Total work = LCM(12,8) = 24 units
A's efficiency = 24/12 = 2 units/day
B's efficiency = 24/8 = 3 units/day
Together = 2+3 = 5 units/day
Days = 24/5 = 4.8 days
⚡ Tricks & Shortcuts
🎯A & B together, B leaves in middle▼

Use LCM method. Find how much B contributes in the days he works, subtract from total. Remaining = A does alone.

A finishes in 10 days, B in 15 days. B leaves after 4 days. How long does A take to finish?
Total = LCM(10,15) = 30. A=3/day, B=2/day.
B's work in 4 days = 4×2 = 8. Together 4 days = 4×(3+2)=20.
Remaining = 30-20 = 10. A alone = 10/3 = 3.33 more days
🔥Pipes & Cisterns (same concept!)▼

Inlet pipes fill (+), outlet pipes empty (−). Just use ± efficiency!

Net rate = Sum of filling rates - Sum of emptying rates
Example: Pipe A fills in 3hr, B fills in 6hr, C empties in 4hr. All open together:
Assume total = 12 units. A=+4, B=+2, C=-3. Net = 3/hr. Time = 12/3 = 4 hours

🚀 Speed, Distance & Time

Trains, boats, races, clocks — all from one master formula.

Master Formula
Distance = Speed × Time
Speed = Distance / Time
Time = Distance / Speed

Convert km/h to m/s: multiply by 5/18
Convert m/s to km/h: multiply by 18/5
⚡ Tricks & Shortcuts
🎯Relative Speed▼
Same direction: Relative Speed = |S1 - S2|
Opposite direction: Relative Speed = S1 + S2
Two trains of length L1 and L2 cross each other:
Time = (L1 + L2) / Relative Speed

Train crosses a stationary object (pole/person):
Time = Train Length / Train Speed
🔥Average Speed (not arithmetic mean!)▼

If same distance at speeds A and B:

Average Speed = 2AB / (A + B) ← Harmonic Mean
Example: 60 km/h going, 40 km/h returning. Average speed?
2×60×40/(60+40) = 4800/100 = 48 km/h (not 50!)
💡Boats & Streams▼
Downstream = B + S (faster)
Upstream = B - S (slower)

Boat speed B = (Down + Up) / 2
Stream speed S = (Down - Up) / 2
Example: Downstream 20 km/h, upstream 12 km/h.
Boat speed = (20+12)/2 = 16 km/h
Stream speed = (20-12)/2 = 4 km/h

📊 Averages

Quick deviations — solve most average problems without adding anything.

Core Formula
Average = Sum of all values / Number of values

Sum = Average × Number of values
⚡ Tricks & Shortcuts
🎯Deviation Method (No addition needed!)▼

Assume any base. Find deviation of each number from base. Average deviation + base = true average.

Find average of 48, 52, 47, 53, 50
Base = 50. Deviations: -2, +2, -3, +3, 0. Sum = 0. Avg deviation = 0.
Average = 50 + 0 = 50
🔥New member joins / one leaves▼
New value added = (New Avg - Old Avg) × New Count + Old Avg
OR: New Member's value = New Average + (Change in avg × new count)
Example: Average of 5 numbers is 20. A 6th number joins, average becomes 22.
6th number = 22×6 - 20×5 = 132 - 100 = 32
💡Wrong entry corrected▼
Correct Sum = Old Sum - Wrong Value + Correct Value
Correct Average = Correct Sum / N
Example: Avg of 10 numbers = 30. One number 52 was read as 25. Correct avg?
Old sum = 300. New sum = 300 - 25 + 52 = 327. Correct avg = 327/10 = 32.7

🔐 Coding-Decoding

Find the pattern and you'll crack it every time.

Types of Coding
🎯Type 1: Letter Shift Coding▼

Each letter is shifted by a fixed number of positions in the alphabet.

Example: If CAT = FDW, find the code for DOG.
C+3=F, A+3=D, T+3=W → shift = +3
D+3=G, O+3=R, G+3=J → DOG = GRJ
🔥Type 2: Reverse Coding (Mirror)▼

Each letter replaced by its mirror: A↔Z, B↔Y, C↔X... Formula: code = 27 - position

If CAT → ZZG? C(3)→X(24)? No. C→X=24, A→Z=26, T→G=7
Pattern: 27 - position. C=3→24=X, A=1→26=Z... so CAT → XZG
💡Type 3: Number-Based Coding▼

Letters replaced by numbers (positions, or position+/-n, or reverse positions)

Example: If BOOK = 2-15-15-11, find CODE.
Each letter = its position value. C=3, O=15, D=4, E=5 → CODE = 3-15-4-5
⚡Type 4: Word Rearrangement Coding▼

Words in a sentence are rearranged or interchanged.

Example: "Sky is blue" coded as "Blue sky is"
Pattern: last word first, then first, then middle.
Code for "Dogs are smart" → "Smart dogs are"

🪑 Seating Arrangement

A systematic approach that solves every puzzle.

Types of Arrangements

Linear Arrangement

People sit in a row. "Left" and "right" are from person's perspective. "Immediate left/right" means adjacent seat.

Circular Arrangement

People sit around a table. "Left" is counter-clockwise, "Right" is clockwise (from person's view). Total arrangements = (n-1)!

⚡ Solving Strategy
🎯Step-by-Step Method▼

1. Draw a blank grid (linear) or circle (circular)
2. Start with definite positions (100% sure clues first)
3. Use elimination — if A can't be here, mark it
4. Work with "adjacent to" clues to fill neighbors
5. For circular: fix one person at top, arrange rest relatively

Example: A, B, C, D, E sit in a row. A is 2nd from left. B is right of C. D is at one end. E is not adjacent to A.
D is at position 1 or 5. A=2. So D=1 or 5. Try D=5. Now B is right of C → CB. Remaining seats 3,4 for B,C,E. E not adjacent to A(pos2) so E≠pos3. E=pos4 or 5(taken). E=4. Then C=3, B=4... conflict. Try again → systematic elimination leads to unique solution.
🔥Circular Seating — Left/Right Rule▼

In a circle facing center: your left is the person to your right from bird's-eye view.

Trick: "A sits to the immediate right of B" means going clockwise from B, next person is A.
